## Implementation Summary

The reports materially improve the first-ingest situation.

Before remediation:

- 30 / 48 targets were ready manual candidates.
- 18 / 48 targets were fallback source stubs.
- fallback rate: 37.5%.

After applying report recommendations as projections, not as final ingests:

- 10 fallback rows are projected replacements after verification.
- 3 fallback rows are projected candidate records after verification.
- 2 rows remain browser/page recheck cases.
- 3 rows remain fallback stubs.
- projected unresolved fallback/recheck rate: 5 / 48 = 10.4%.

This meets the earlier goal of reducing the first production fallback rate below 15%, provided the replacement/promote rows pass source-level verification.

## Remaining Caveat

The source expansion table is still uneven.

It is strong for:

- Western/Central Europe;
- Eastern Europe;
- North America;
- Latin America;
- East Asia;
- Southeast Asia;
- global digital/web sources.

It is still weak or absent as a structured source table for:

- South Asia beyond NID-style institutional records;
- MENA beyond Iranian poster/design context;
- Africa beyond Medu/SAHA-style cases;
- Oceania and Indigenous Australian sources beyond NAIDOC/AIATSIS-type protocol-sensitive cases.

Therefore, this report is good enough to improve the first ingest pipeline, but not enough to declare global source coverage complete.

## Recommended Next Move

Do not immediately crawl everything.

Use this sequence:

1. Verify the 13 projected promote/replace fallback rows.
2. Convert verified replacements into manual source record drafts.
3. Keep the remaining 5 unresolved rows as fallback/browser-recheck stubs.
4. Promote only a controlled subset of the 20 low-friction production sources into `source_registry`.
5. Run a new under-covered-region source audit for South Asia, MENA, Africa, Oceania/Indigenous, and non-institutional/community sources.

This preserves the archive rule: Deep Research produces candidate evidence; source-level verification promotes it.
