## Bottom line

The comparison rejects scale, browse/search, public APIs, imagery, responsive visual presentation, or even a generically "open archive" as a defensible novelty claim for `graphic_design_archive`. Mature collection and aggregator systems already offer those capabilities at substantially larger scales: Rijksmuseum reports metadata for 800,000 objects, Cooper Hewitt API 215,000 items, Smithsonian 5.1m digital items, Letterform Archive 100,000 physical/digital holdings, and AIGA 20,000+ selections [S01–S06].

The potentially distinctive proposition is much narrower: **a release-pinned, evidence-bounded navigation layer that makes the difference between corpus membership/context and an accepted object-to-object historical claim visible, and refuses to infer influence from visual similarity, co-presence, medium, date, or place.** This is a *candidate* differentiation, not yet a demonstrated field-first: the inspected competitors generally do not document enough about their internal claim/relation review to support a universal absence claim. It becomes defensible only after accepted claims actually exist and their source/review/provenance policy is published and enforced.

The evidence also argues strongly against calling TRACE an *influence map*. Pinterest and Cosmos expressly foreground visual similarity/discovery [S14–S15], while museum/aggregator records foreground custody, rights, record provenance, identifiers, and/or contributor links [S01–S11]. Similarity-led discovery is a useful interaction reference, but it is not historical evidence. With `TRACE_ELIGIBLE_OBJECTS=0`, an honest public state is **TRACE Preview / Evidence Trace (no accepted relations in this release)**, with Atlas and research-path/membership views distinguished from claims.

### Matrix reading notes

1. **Custody is a material distinction.** First-party museums control at least part of their record/image pipeline; Europeana and DPLA are explicit aggregators/federators; the proposed project should call itself a *visual federation* only when it preserves the source and rights context per displayed item. An external URL or thumbnail is not a licence. Europeana’s APIs expose rights-bearing records and DPLA describes an item as a reference to a digital representation in a provider repository [S16–S19].
2. **Metadata licence is not image licence.** MoMA releases collection metadata CC0 while explicitly excluding images, and says much of that data is incomplete and not curatorially approved [S05–S06]. Smithsonian distinguishes CC0 assets from restricted materials and may withhold media URLs where rights limit access [S07–S08]. These are direct precedents for separating object status, media availability, and permissions.
3. **Relations are not automatically claims.** Cooper Hewitt exposes terms/tags; Rijksmuseum exposes controlled vocabularies/Linked Data; DPLA exposes item/collection structure. None of these official descriptions makes a licensed inference from a visual or taxonomic association to historical influence [S03–S04, S09–S11, S18–S19]. Do not label taxonomy, research-tree membership, similar imagery, or co-occurrence as TRACE evidence.
4. **Useful comparator, not standard bearer.** Pinterest and Cosmos can inform lightweight collection, browsing, visual-similarity and source-discovery interactions only. Their inspected pages document no scholarly evidence/review contract; they cannot substantiate a design-history contribution [S25–S26].
5. **Do not overclaim competitor absence.** `UNKNOWN` in a row (especially claim/evidence separation, review status, accessibility, citation, release versioning and missingness) reflects public-documentation limits. It must never be transformed to `NO` in the main synthesis without project-specific proof.

## Competitive interpretation for TRACE

### What the project can competitively claim, conditional on implementation evidence

- **A. Defensible core contribution (conditional):** a release-pinned interface that traverses *coverage → curated research membership → object-level, source-cited claims*, exposing evidence/uncertainty/provenance and prohibiting automated influence inference. This must be released with claim state and source/review fields, not just visual layout.
- **B. Supporting research contribution:** explicit missingness, contextual/orphan evidence and "held" states. The direct precedent is MoMA’s honest incomplete/not-curatorially-approved warning, but TRACE’s proposed finer-grained public epistemic status would be a useful extension if real accepted records are supplied [S06].
- **C. Infrastructure contribution:** versioned, read-only, machine-readable release with stable IDs, rights state and source links. This is useful and comparable to V&A, Cooper Hewitt, Rijksmuseum, DPLA, Europeana and Trove; it is infrastructure, **not unique novelty** [S01–S04, S09–S11, S16–S20].
- **D. Product/education value:** macro-to-micro wayfinding and rights-aware source federation, provided every view says what it encodes. Letterform, AIGA, Smithsonian and Google all already show the educational/public value of curated access [S07, S12–S15, S22–S24].
- **E. Portfolio signal:** complex responsive graphics, map/constellation interaction, schema discipline, provenance UI, API integration, CI/release process and operational maintenance. These are good evidence of ability, not research contributions by themselves.
- **F. Common/non-contribution:** 15,923 objects; target 20,000; search/filter; generic API; external images; generic collection pages; graph visualisation; "AI-free" visual browsing; mobile responsiveness. Competitors show that each exists independently at scale [S01–S20, S25–S26].
- **G. Unsupported/prohibited now:** "influence map", "maps design influence", "evidence graph" without accepted edges, "rights-cleared images" merely because URLs are external, "complete/representative modern graphic design history", "first/only", or claims that visual similarity/temporal/geographic/medium proximity proves relation.

### Specific product consequences

| Surface | It may say now | It must not imply |
|---|---|---|
| Global Atlas | release-pinned corpus distribution, filters and documented coverage/missingness | that a density, cluster or absence represents design-history causality or completeness |
| Evidence Constellation | curated research-tree/group membership and navigation path | membership is an influence, lineage, citation or verified relation |
| Object Trace | object metadata, sources, rights/media state, and **no accepted relationship claims in this release** | a source list, related-items list or image similarity establishes a claim |
| Image federation | an external source is linked/displayed under a recorded availability/rights state | the archive owns, licenses, preserves, or can sublicense the image |

At zero accepted TRACE, deploy any graph-like view only as **non-relational research-path preview** with a visible count and legend. The strongest short title is **Evidence Trace**; preserve the word TRACE only if its expansion and status notice are on-screen. Do not launch a relation-bearing “Constellation” in a way a reasonable visitor reads as history of influence.

## Comparative gaps and two-week priorities

The relevant deficit versus mature archives is not animation; it is demonstrable, maintainable stewardship: collection policy/custody, granular rights, identifiers, reliable item provenance, user-facing citations, governed corrections, preservation/sustainability and actual reviewed claims. Letterform’s specialist curatorial custody, Rijksmuseum’s persistent data architecture, Smithsonian’s rights separation, and DPLA/Europeana’s contributor-source paths make this visible [S07–S13, S16–S19].

For the next two weeks, the minimum credible release is:

1. Freeze a release identifier and publish the baseline counts, including `TRACE_ELIGIBLE_OBJECTS=0`, `HELD_OBJECTS=7928`, and the explicit old-v48-graph exclusion.
2. Make each view distinguish **distribution**, **membership**, **context/source**, **claim**, **held**, and **unknown/missing** in copy, legend, data exports and accessible table equivalents.
3. Add per-record source URL/citation and rights/media wording that does not imply permission; preserve the upstream institution/provider in every export.
4. Publish a short no-inference and relation-publication threshold policy (independent provenance roots, claim type, source/review record, contradiction/qualification state).
5. Do not spend the window on 20,000 objects, automatic/LLM/embedding relation discovery, v48 repair, 3D/WebGL, ontology/RDF completion, or cosmetic visual expansion. Any new objects should be accepted only when they improve evidence coverage, source-family diversity, regional/temporal bias documentation, or rights/provenance quality.
