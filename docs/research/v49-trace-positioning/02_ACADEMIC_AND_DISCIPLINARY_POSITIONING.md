# Academic and disciplinary positioning

## Field decision

The primary field is **Digital Humanities (DH), specifically design-history digital scholarship**. The relevant research question is not how to optimise a collection UI; it is how historical-design research can make selection, transformation, source context, qualification and missingness inspectable while using computation and visual navigation. DH scholarship treats interpretation, data and visualisation as entangled rather than as a neutral display layer [SR01][SR02].

The two secondary positions are: (1) **digital heritage / archival-information science**, for accountable selection, digital provenance, source context, rights status and visible lacunae; and (2) **humanities visual analytics/HCI**, for the interaction and comprehension design of uncertainty-aware evidence navigation [SR05][SR17]. Research-software engineering is an infrastructure framing, not the project’s central scholarly field [SR07].

## What follows from that decision

The archive is a research index and interface, not a custodial museum archive or digital-preservation repository. External visual federation means preservation and image custody remain with source institutions. A curated research tree is a project representation, not “original archival order.” No current evidence supports claims of corpus-wide representativeness, permission to reuse imagery, user comprehension, or a distinct visual-analytics method; each is `UNKNOWN` until evidenced [SR05][SR06][SR25].

The appropriate near-term scholarly form is a **DH research-infrastructure/design case study**. A future data paper can document selection policy, coverage, provenance, licences/rights states and reuse. A future system/design-case paper needs an accepted, source-linked claim corpus and a study showing that users can trace evidence and avoid false inference. A design-history article requires a bounded historical question and historian-led source argument; scale alone cannot produce it.

## Standards-informed, not standards-compliant

CIDOC CRM/CRMinf, W3C PROV and Web Annotation justify keeping object, evidence, claim, review and provenance distinct. IIIF can inform an outbound holder/manifest link where actually available; Linked Art supports a small vocabulary and stable identifiers. None warrants claims of CRM/PROV/IIIF/Linked Art conformance, an RDF/triple-store programme, or automated historical reasoning [SR03][SR08][SR09][SR10].

## Contribution wording

Safe research wording:

> A design-history DH case study that tests a release-pinned, evidence-bounded route from corpus coverage to curated research pathways and source-level object dossiers, with explicit missingness and a no-inference rule.

This must stay conditional while `TRACE_ELIGIBLE_OBJECTS=0`. The project can demonstrate a method contract and honest interface state; it cannot yet demonstrate a relation dataset, relation-navigation outcome, historical network finding or influence analysis.

## Evidence needed to promote the core claim

1. Publish predicate-specific acceptance, independence and review rules before claims.
2. Release a small deliberate gold set with stable claim/relation/evidence IDs, exact locators, independent provenance roots where required, qualifications and review decisions.
3. Publish a negative/held disposition for rejected candidates rather than disguising it as empty UI.
4. Evaluate researcher/curator tasks: source recovery, qualification comprehension, false-inference rate, keyboard/mobile access.
5. Document coverage and bias by era, region, language, institution, source family and medium before making any corpus-level historical statement [SR02][SR16][SR17].
