# Competitive gaps and red-team decision

## Competitive result

The 15-project comparison shows mature institutions already offer high-volume collections, search/filter, APIs, linked data, citations, visual discovery, image delivery and education. Examples include Cooper Hewitt’s 215,000-item API, Rijksmuseum’s 800,000 metadata records, Smithsonian’s 5.1m digital items, Letterform Archive’s 100,000 holdings, and AIGA’s 20,000+ selections [SR11][SR13][SR14][SR21]. These are not harmonised counts and not a ranking, but they decisively reject count/API/search/image claims as differentiation.

The conditional differentiation is narrower: an inspectable distinction among coverage, editorial membership, evidence occurrence, claim, review/provenance and projection, coupled with a publicly enforceable no-inference policy. Existing competitors’ public documentation is often insufficient to prove the absence of such methods, so do not claim “first” or “only.”

## Most serious gaps

1. **No accepted TRACE substrate.** A relation/influence layer has zero accepted objects; current release cannot establish an empirical relation result.
2. **Coverage unknowns.** No demonstrated representativeness or documented region/era/source-family/medium/custody distributions. Large digitised aggregates can reproduce collection bias [SR19][SR20].
3. **Non-custodial rights limit.** External access does not establish display/reuse/training rights or preservation stewardship [SR23][SR24].
4. **Sustainability unknown.** No evidenced succession, funder, correction SLA, source refresh or preservation commitment; do not imply an institutional archive [SR28].
5. **Visual-authority hazard.** Complexity can conceal a curated or incomplete substrate; evaluation must test false inference, not visual engagement [SR02][SR17].

## Red-team verdict

**GO-NARROW.** The narrow release can be credible only as an evidence-navigation/corpus-coverage prototype with full zero-state and rights/missingness language. It becomes **NO-GO** if it retains relation-like v48 visuals, uses influence/network rhetoric, substitutes membership/candidate counts for accepted relations, or presents third-party visuals as cleared/custodied. A full GO needs a sealed, audited claim pilot, accessible verification and a maintenance card.

## Executable remediation

P0: freeze count/policy/release ID; name the zero state; distinguish layer semantics in legend/export; retain source/provider/rights access date; table/list/reduced-motion route; no expansion.

P1: small non-causal claim pilot only if every claim has wording, source locator, provenance-root assessment, review decision, qualification/contradiction and public-safe rights state; curator/historian review it.

P2: task-test source recovery, membership-vs-relation comprehension and unavailable-state comprehension across desktop/mobile/keyboard/screen reader; publish a maintenance/takedown/correction card.

## Prohibited public claims

“Influence map/network”; “discovers hidden relationships”; “automatically infers influence”; “comprehensive/global/representative archive/history”; “15,923 unique works”; “20,000 quality threshold”; “rights-cleared/open image archive”; “museum-grade”; “authoritative”; “peer-reviewed”; “production-ready”; “accessible”; “reproducible”; and “preserves the collection.” Use them only if their distinct evidenced conditions have been independently demonstrated.
