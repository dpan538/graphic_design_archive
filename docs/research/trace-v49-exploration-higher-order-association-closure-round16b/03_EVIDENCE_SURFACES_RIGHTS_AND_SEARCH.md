# Evidence surfaces, rights, and adaptive search

## Governed local surface inventory

`raw/evidence-surface-inventory.tsv` hashes and counts the Round 9–16A research, evidence, negative-control, product, and database surfaces in scope. It corrects a material Round 16A omission: the pair census input inventory did not include the Round 10 role/arity, grammar-attestation, cluster, and chain registries or the Round 15 and legacy multi-node composition surfaces.

The inventory assigns an authority boundary to every surface. Important distinctions include:

- bibliographic identity versus locator-bearing evidence;
- concept support versus association support;
- pair decision versus group decision;
- synthetic control versus historical evidence;
- inactive inquiry package versus active authority;
- database provenance discovery versus scholarly evidence;
- prior product realization versus globally coherent association.

The frozen database is a discovery surface only. Object folders, categories, similarity, TRACE edges, shared sources, and rights-state records may identify a case or source family for follow-up. They never validate a Function 3 association by themselves.

## Scholarly source access and retention

The existing scholarly source registries do not record license, access condition, copyright holder, or redistribution authority. Public availability, a DOI, a PDF link, or repository access does not itself authorize redistribution. Archive-object rights fields do not govern scholarly-text rights.

`raw/scholarly-source-rights-policy.json` permits committing bibliographic identity, stable locators, retrieval timestamps, access conditions, license/rights decisions, hashes of lawfully accessed material, bounded research notes, compliant short extracts, and review decisions. Copyrighted full texts, publisher PDFs, ebooks, chapters, or bulk proprietary content must not be committed without explicit redistribution authority.

Every supporting source needs a row in the Round 16B rights/access ledger. If legitimate review cannot be completed, the candidate remains inquiry-only or access-blocked. No access workaround is permitted.

The committed `raw/scholarly-source-rights-ledger.tsv` is an empty governed template at this checkpoint. It must be populated for every source used by a candidate review; its current emptiness is evidence that no supporting source has been prematurely activated.

## Adaptive external discovery protocol

External search begins only from a governed trigger or recursive gap. One quoted all-term query is never treated as a complete multi-term review. A candidate search shard may include:

1. the complete concept group and attested variants;
2. historically meaningful subsets;
3. source-centred and citation-chain follow-up;
4. case, institution, period, geography, actor, medium, movement, and mechanism constraints;
5. counterexample, incompatibility, and falsification queries.

Each query records its normalized text, timestamp, service, parameters, result identity, access condition, decision, stable locator, and rejection reason. Metadata results remain discovery-only until a legitimate evidence-bearing passage or deposited abstract is reviewed at a stable locator. Search shards are resumable, deterministic after freeze, and predeclared for LFS when needed.

## Search decision classes

Every result must be assigned one of the following outcomes or a more specific governed child class:

- evidence locator accepted for review;
- metadata discovery only;
- concept or co-occurrence only;
- incompatible case or scope;
- bounded-sense conflict;
- topology or role conflict;
- duplicate source identity;
- rights or access blocked;
- irrelevant to the exact candidate;
- counterevidence or hard negative.

Search completion is not measured by result volume. It is measured by trigger coverage, source decisions, locator-bearing review, counterevidence review, and a final candidate disposition.
