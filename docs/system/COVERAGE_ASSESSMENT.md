# Coverage Assessment v0

**Status:** Critical coverage note.  
**Date:** 2026-05-29

## Short Answer

No. The current framework does not completely cover all events in the development history of graphic design.

It should not claim to do so.

The current framework is a structured, rights-aware, reproducible skeleton for indexing modern graphic design history. It provides a starting historical spine, taxonomy, source universe, rights strategy, and search vocabulary. It is sufficient to begin database validation and later controlled source-record entry, but it is not a complete event history.

## Revised Coverage Commitment

The project must pursue broad coverage across Europe, the Americas, Japan, Korea, East Asia, Mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and other global regions. This is not a decorative inclusion requirement. It is a structural requirement.

If the framework remains primarily Euro-American and only adds a few non-Western examples, it fails its own methodology.

Coverage must therefore be tracked as a database problem:

- region list;
- region-node coverage matrix;
- regional source priorities;
- regional search vocabulary;
- regional event layer;
- regional gap notes;
- authority resolution by language and jurisdiction.

## Why Complete Coverage Is Not Possible

Graphic design history is not a closed event list. It includes:

- formal design movements;
- printing and media technologies;
- commercial production;
- anonymous and collective labor;
- local job printing;
- advertising;
- newspapers and magazines;
- book and publication design;
- packaging;
- signage;
- identity systems;
- political and activist graphics;
- design education;
- exhibitions and awards;
- software and interfaces;
- vernacular design;
- community and subcultural production;
- platform-native visual communication;
- born-digital and ephemeral materials.

Many of these materials were not collected by museums or archives. Many were temporary, anonymous, commercially owned, privately held, poorly cataloged, or never digitized. A claim of complete coverage would violate the project's integrity principle.

## What the Current Framework Does Cover

The current seed layer provides broad coverage across:

- print preconditions;
- movable type and early printing;
- engraving, etching, and reproductive print;
- lithography and chromolithography;
- nineteenth-century commercial print;
- Arts and Crafts, Art Nouveau, Jugendstil, and Secession;
- avant-garde experiment;
- Bauhaus and New Typography;
- propaganda and public information;
- Swiss Style, grids, systems, wayfinding, and information design;
- corporate identity, editorial systems, and packaging systems;
- protest graphics and counterpublics;
- postmodern, vernacular, punk, zines, and independent publishing;
- phototypesetting, dry transfer, DTP, and digital type;
- web, interface, platform, algorithmic, and generative communication.

It also includes seed taxonomies for:

- 38 movements/formations;
- 35 media/technology/format terms;
- 35 source candidates;
- 163 search vocabulary terms;
- 10 rights strategies.

## Major Known Gaps

### Geographic Gaps

The seed framework still over-relies on Euro-American reference points. It has some global hooks, but it needs much more detail for:

- Mainland China;
- Hong Kong;
- Taiwan;
- Korea;
- Japan beyond poster/design canon;
- East Asia as a transnational circulation frame;
- South Asia;
- Southeast Asia;
- Africa beyond selected diasporic and postcolonial frames;
- Latin America beyond major poster and modernist references;
- Middle East and North Africa;
- Eastern Europe and socialist design systems;
- Indigenous design histories across different territories;
- Oceania and Pacific visual communication.

### Labor and Production Gaps

The framework needs stronger categories for:

- printers;
- typesetters;
- engravers;
- sign painters;
- paste-up workers;
- production artists;
- service bureaus;
- copy shops;
- newspaper layout workers;
- anonymous commercial studios;
- non-elite design labor.

### Commercial and Vernacular Gaps

Much graphic design history sits outside canonical design archives:

- local advertising;
- shop signs;
- packaging;
- labels;
- trade catalogues;
- receipts and tickets;
- menus;
- forms;
- business stationery;
- religious print;
- tourism graphics;
- local political pamphlets;
- amateur and small-business graphics.

These are only lightly represented in the seed taxonomy.

### Born-Digital Gaps

The current framework names web/interface/platform history but does not yet provide a full event map for:

- early web design cultures;
- browser-specific visual conventions;
- Flash-era design;
- blog themes;
- CMS templates;
- mobile app visual systems;
- design systems;
- social media templates;
- meme and platform-native graphic formats;
- generative and AI-assisted workflows.

### Textual and Discourse Gaps

The project still needs stronger indexing for:

- design criticism;
- manifestos;
- trade journals;
- exhibition catalogues;
- course syllabi;
- design manuals;
- software manuals;
- corporate standards documents;
- oral histories;
- interviews;
- local-language design writing.

### Event-Level Gaps

The current framework is node-level, not event-level. It does not yet list all:

- exhibitions;
- school openings and closures;
- journal launches;
- publication dates;
- poster campaigns;
- corporate identity launches;
- software releases;
- typeface releases;
- conferences;
- awards;
- protests and campaigns;
- policy or institutional events.

An event layer must be built later from sources and citations.

## Recommended Coverage Method

The project should track coverage through explicit gap-aware fields:

- geographic coverage;
- date range coverage;
- source type coverage;
- movement coverage;
- media/technology coverage;
- rights availability;
- digitization bias;
- authority availability;
- underdocumented notes.

Coverage should be measured by what has been indexed and cited, not by what the framework names.

## Coverage Rule

The project may say:

> This framework provides a structured entry point into modern graphic design history and is designed to expand through cited source records.

The project must not say:

> This framework fully covers graphic design history.
