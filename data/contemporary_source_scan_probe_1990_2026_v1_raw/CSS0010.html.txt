    <h2 class="post__title h1">
        <a class="builtin" href="https://letterformarchive.org/news/the-small-foundry-fund-for-type-west/" title="View Post: The Small Foundry Fund for Type West at Letterform Archive">The Small Foundry Fund for Type West at Letterform Archive</a>
    </h2>

            <h3 class="post__subtitle h2"><p>Introducing a new community-driven funding initiative devoted to expanding access to our Type West program.</p>
</h3>
        <div class="post__excerpt">
        <figure class="L"><img decoding="async" src="https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/LetterForm-Archive-33-e1777590880117.jpg" loading="lazy"></figure>

<figure class="S"><img decoding="async" src="https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Type-West-logo.png" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p>We are excited to announce a meaningful way to support <a href="https://letterformarchive.org/education/#type-west">Type West</a> students through accessible and high-impact giving. What’s Type West? Type West is a yearlong, postgraduate certificate program in type design available in person in San Francisco or online to students worldwide. We are exceedingly proud of this program, which is now <a href="https://letterformarchive.org/news/type-west-alumni-spotlight-2024-2025/">celebrating its tenth year</a>!</p>

    </h2>

            <h3 class="post__subtitle h2"><p>We return to Switzerland to consider the impact of the provocative designer who pushed modern typography to its limits.</p>
</h3>
        <div class="post__excerpt">
        <figure class="XL"><img decoding="async" src="https://letterformarchive.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/weingart-banner-full.jpg" loading="lazy"></figure><p>In 1972, the editorial board of <a href: "https://letterformarchive.org/news/legacies-of-swiss-style-tyografische-monatsblatter/"><cite><u>Typografische Monatsblätter (TM)</cite></u></a>, one of the leading trade journals in Switzerland, approved a series of cover designs intended to lay the groundwork for the publication’s new artistic direction. Enlisting newly appointed board member Wolfgang Weingart (1941–2021) to produce fifteen covers for the 1972 and ’73 print runs, the outcome inspired both high praise and harsh criticism from a design milieu accustomed to the quiet precision of former art director Robert Büchler (1914–2005) and regular contributor Emil Ruder (1914–1970). Weingart’s provocative covers invited an unprecedented level of controversy by appearing to flout the fundamental principles of modernist design. Instead, his iconoclastic approach struck at the very heart of the Swiss tradition.</p>    </div>
